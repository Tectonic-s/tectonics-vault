# Taste

Taste is the ability to make the *right* decision when there is no rule — and, more often, the discipline to **remove** rather than add. Good taste is invisible: nothing calls attention to itself, yet the whole feels inevitable, calm, and expensive.

## The one question

Before adding anything, ask: **"What can I remove and still keep the meaning?"** Most designs improve by subtraction. A border you can delete, a shadow you can soften, a color you can drop, a word you can cut — do it.

## Principles

1. **Restraint over decoration.** No gradient, glow, shadow, icon, or divider earns its place by default. Each must justify itself. When in doubt, leave it out. One accent, one idea per screen.
2. **Hierarchy is everything.** The eye should always know where to look first, second, third. Create it with *size, weight, and space* before reaching for color or rules. If two things compete, one must yield.
3. **Whitespace is confidence.** Generous, intentional negative space reads as premium and calm; cramped space reads as cheap and anxious. Don't fill the canvas — frame the content.
4. **Everything on a system.** Spacing, type sizes, radii, and durations come from a small scale, not arbitrary values. Coherence is felt even when it can't be named. Ad-hoc numbers are the tell of amateur work.
5. **Type does the heavy lifting.** A single family with a real size/weight scale beats a pile of styles. Set generous line-height for body (1.5–1.8), tight for display. Optical alignment over mathematical. Punctuation and casing are details that signal care.
6. **Color is a scalpel.** Monochrome + one warm/functional accent almost always beats a palette. Use color to mean something (state, emphasis), never to entertain. Respect contrast for legibility.
7. **Alignment and rhythm.** Everything lines up to a grid or an edge. Consistent vertical rhythm between groups makes a page feel composed rather than assembled.
8. **Kill the clichés.** Avoid the default of the moment (glassmorphism spam, rainbow gradients, drop-shadow soup, emoji-as-UI, hero blob). Trendy dates fast; restraint is timeless.
9. **Details are the design.** 1px seams, inconsistent radii, a hover that jumps, text that reflows, a misaligned baseline — these are what separate "fine" from "impeccable." Sweat them.
10. **Reference the best, don't copy.** Internalize why Apple, Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Aesop, and Kinfolk feel the way they do — quiet confidence, editorial spacing, honest materials — then make your own coherent choice.

## The taste checklist

- Can I remove an element, a color, a font, a line, a shadow?
- Is the hierarchy unmistakable in a 3-second squint test?
- Does the spacing come from a scale, and does it *breathe*?
- Is there exactly one accent doing one job?
- Would this still look right in a year, or is it chasing a trend?
- Do the small things (radii, baselines, casing, alignment) all agree?

If a change makes it quieter, more spacious, and more legible — it's almost always right.
